# Scientific outreach

Text another researcher reads and judges. The failure modes are sounding machine-written, and sounding like a pitch.

## The posted text itself

- Concise, itemised
- Bullet lists, not prose paragraphs
- One short lead-in, then bullets
- Applies to comments, proposals, emails

## Voice

- Never reads as AI-generated
- No marketing, no CV register
- Sentence lengths uneven
- No em-dashes, tricolons, stacked colons
- Flagged paragraph: rewrite, never patch the quoted phrase

## Banned phrasings

- "Some of this exists"
- "you are welcome to it"
- "I would push it further"
- "the durable deliverable is"
- "One constraint:"

Also: "the property worth more than", "the awkward part", "One thing to settle early", "rather than a side project"

## Social

- Lead with theirs, not ours
- Affirm before correcting
- Cross-link others' related work
- Verdicts become recommendations
- Conflict of interest in the first lines

## Asks

- One question, cheap to answer
- Assets last, under the offer
- Drop cc'd people once introduced
- Offer preparation, not attendance
- Name the constraint, ask nothing in return

## Never

- Cite their step or section numbers
- Praise their metrics or scores in the opening
- Claim novelty on their behalf
- Promise a repo that does not exist
- Link a URL without checking it resolves

## Process

- Pull the target text before drafting; verify against it, not memory
- Check sibling proposals or threads; cross-links are the cheapest goodwill
- Quality named ("too long", "AI slop"): regenerate the passage
- Same passage flagged twice: stop, ask what the intended outcome is
- Technical depth goes in a linked doc or the call, not the post
